# Simplified Chinese narration notes

## Scope

`zh-Hans.json` is a complete Mandarin adaptation of the supplied English adaptation, not a new translation of the Sanskrit verses. All reader-facing titles, subtitles, dialogue, explanations, examples, contextual notes, chapter explanations, reflections, and speaker labels are translated. The original order, IDs, verse references, ranges, Sanskrit chapter titles, and production metadata are retained.

The prose uses modern, dignified spoken Mandarin. Punctuation supplies natural pauses; it does not require reading labels, quotation marks, or identifiers aloud. No audio has been generated. The pronunciation guidance below is intended for later voice direction and pronunciation dictionaries.

## Om, Tat, Sat

The spoken Chinese text uses **嗡、塔特、萨特**. Chinese character readings are **wēng, tǎ tè, sà tè**. These are narration aids, not an exact Sanskrit phonetic transcription. If the eventual voice supports a pronunciation override, use a sustained **Om** and crisp **Tat, Sat**, approximately `/oːm/`, `/t̪ət̪/`, `/sət̪/`; do not substitute English letter names. This note does not add a chant or prescribe repetition. Chapter 8 and chapter 17 use the same 嗡 rendering.

## Material ambiguities and interpretive limits

- The source is an adaptation with compressed verse groups and one explicitly marked bridging beat in scene 10.4. The bridge remains translated and its `bridge: true` flag remains unchanged. The Chinese should not be advertised as a word-for-word canonical Sanskrit translation.
- Dharma has no single adequate Chinese equivalent here. The transliteration 达摩 is retained where the philosophical term itself matters, with responsibility, conduct, and sustaining order explained in the surrounding prose. It does not refer to the monk Bodhidharma in this script. The contested conclusion “relinquish all dharmas” remains **放下一切达摩**, and the source's warning against reducing it to arbitrary rule-breaking or abandonment of care is preserved.
- 真我, 梵, 个体真我, and 至上者 are kept distinct. The source leaves room for differing traditions on the relation of the individual knower, Brahman, and Krishna; the translation does not settle those debates. **至上者** in chapter 15 retains a personal divine meaning and should not be read as a generic successful person.
- Social hierarchy remains contextualized in chapters 1, 4, 9, and 18. Mentions of status, birth, gender, outcasting, and the four varnas are not modernized into an unqualified equality claim or “follow your passion.” The source's external context-note pointers remain pointers; no missing note was invented inside this JSON.
- The divine-care passage at 9.22 remains a devotional assurance, with its explicit limitation against treating it as a promise of financial success or exemption from loss.
- Chapter 11 retains Krishna's command within the epic's particular setting and preserves the warning against using it to justify present-day violence. The four-armed requested form and the subsequent gentle human form both remain, following the source's sequence without inventing an additional transition.
- Rebirth, death, cosmic cycles, liberation, and the paths after death remain religious claims. Everyday examples are retained as limited analogies, not replacements for those claims.
- 三德 is a conventional philosophical collective term whose 德 means a quality here, not an assertion that all three are moral virtues. The text retains the source's ethical, psychological, and cosmological dimensions and its rejection of medical/personality-type readings.
- Om/Tat/Sat pronunciation in an eventual synthetic voice needs an audio check. This deliverable contains text and pronunciation direction only; no claim of ElevenLabs synthesis or voice verification is made.
